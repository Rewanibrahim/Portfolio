Happy Tails

Pet care mobile app & landing page · Route UI/UX Diploma, 2025

One app for a pet's food, vet visits and vaccination dates, shaped around what owners told me about their daily routines.

Role
UI/UX designer, solo
Research
3 interviews, survey of 15 owners
Output
18 screens, clickable prototype, landing page

The problem

Owners juggle feeding, vaccines, grooming and vet visits across different apps, forget reminders, and struggle to track several pets at once. No app combined pet shopping, health tracking and service booking.

How I worked

  1. Listen. Interviewed 3 pet owners and surveyed 15, then synthesized the findings into personas, empathy maps and affinity maps.
  2. Decide. Turned the research into three core features: pet profiles, vaccination reminders and pet-supply shopping.
  3. Map. Built the full user flow in FigJam: onboarding, sign in, sign up or continue as a guest, browse by pet, product, cart, and payment by card, Apple Pay or cash on delivery.
  4. Design. 18 high-fidelity screens linked into a clickable prototype, plus a marketing landing page.

Closet

Fashion e-commerce mobile app · Route UI/UX Diploma, 2025

A calm, warm shopping app that takes people from first launch to a placed order in as few decisions as possible.

Role
UI/UX designer, solo
Scope
18 screens, onboarding to checkout
System
Reusable component library

What shoppers told me

I interviewed two young shoppers with 22 questions each, then mapped what I heard into an empathy map, an affinity map, a persona, a SWOT and a journey map in FigJam.

"Sometimes the quality doesn't match the pictures, or I can't find my size."Shopper, 20
"Good reviews and real customer feedback" is what makes them trust an app.Both shoppers

Three pain points kept coming up:

  • Inconsistent sizing
  • Poor filtering
  • Unrealistic product photos

And one need shaped the whole structure: shopping for the whole family in one place, not women-only or kids-only apps.

What I did

  • Ran a competitive analysis of fashion apps and created proto-personas to define the information architecture for women's, men's and kids' categories.
  • Designed the full journey: onboarding, sign in, browsing, favorites, product details, cart, checkout and order tracking.
  • Put size and color choice right on the product page, and offered cash on delivery at checkout, since both shoppers said they pay cash.
  • Built a component library for product cards, filters and navigation, with shared color and type styles.

How a project moves

The same path every time, scaled to the project.

  1. Research

    Interviews, surveys and competitive analysis to find what people actually struggle with.

  2. Synthesize

    Personas, empathy and affinity maps that turn notes into decisions.

  3. Structure

    Information architecture and user flows before any pixels.

  4. Design

    High-fidelity screens built from a component library, linked into a prototype.

  5. Hand off

    Dev Mode-ready files, and a designer who can read the code.
